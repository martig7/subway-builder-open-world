# Japan non-commute inter-prefecture demand from e-Stat and MLIT

Date: 2026-09-11. Scope: data evaluation and implementation recommendation; no source code or generated demand was changed.

## Conclusion

The best official control for adding domestic non-commute cross-tile demand is the **2015 Sixth National Survey of Net Passenger Flow** (全国幹線旅客純流動調査), using its **residence-to-travel-destination, 207-living-area OD tables**, not its coarser prefecture tables. It measures the intended concepts—business trips, tourism, private/homecoming, and other travel while excluding commuting and schooling—and provides weekday, holiday, mode, and purpose views. e-Stat currently exposes the Sixth Survey as downloadable Excel/CSV tables; the 2020 Seventh Survey was cancelled, and MLIT conducted Eighth Survey fieldwork in October 2025 but has not published a newer result in the catalog. [e-Stat catalog](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/index.php/stat-search/files?kikan=00600&layout=normal&page=1&toukei=00600465&tstat=000001218540), [2025 Eighth Survey announcement](https://www.mlit.go.jp/report/press/sogo23_hh_000184.html), [rail component history](https://www.mlit.go.jp/statistics/details/t-tetsudo-2_tk_000027.html)

The user's proposed prefecture-to-prefecture control followed by population-density allocation is directionally sound, but it leaves official precision unused and gives poor destination locations. A stronger version is:

1. Conserve each directed **207-zone-to-207-zone** flow, split by purpose and representative/used mode as needed.
2. Allocate origins within the origin zone from the project's origin-side distribution, upgraded from the current worker/student marginal to total residential population where the source represents resident trip production.
3. Allocate destinations from the project's existing endpoint/worker distribution, not from the origin population surface. Purpose-specific evidence should refine that endpoint surface only where it measures destination intensity—for example, employment/commercial activity for business and accommodation/attractions for tourism; residential population is justified for the home endpoint of private/homecoming travel, not as the generic destination fallback.
4. Treat the missing metropolitan-area pairs as a separate source problem. The national trunk survey intentionally omits flows within the Tokyo, Chukyo, and Kinki metropolitan groupings, so Tokyo–Kanagawa and the other internal metro prefecture pairs are structural zeroes, not measured zero demand. [MLIT zone definition](https://www.mlit.go.jp/sogoseisaku/soukou/content/001340155.pdf)

This source produces **zone-to-zone estimates, not point-to-point observations**. The 207 zones are groupings of municipalities and are explicitly described as smaller than a prefecture but larger than a municipality. Some are still very coarse: the published zone correspondence groups all 23 Tokyo wards into one `23区` zone. Any building, station, or coordinate endpoint is therefore synthetic and must not be presented as observed. [MLIT survey summary](https://www.mlit.go.jp/common/001297857.pdf), [published 207-zone correspondence](https://www.mlit.go.jp/seisakutokatsu/jyunryuudou/doc/207_Zone2005.pdf)

## Current repository baseline

The runnable national consumer is manifest `local.japan-open-world`, built from [`prototype/japan/mod`](../../prototype/japan/mod/manifest.json). Its World declares the `estat-japan` adapter and the source set `japan-estat-national-2020-2021` in [`worlds/japan/demand.json`](../../worlds/japan/demand.json).

Current demand is commute/school demand only:

- Observed OD controls are 2020 Census Table 6-1 residence-municipality to workplace/school-municipality flows.
- Origin marginals are 2020 Census 250 m mesh residents aged 15+ who work or study.
- Destination capacity is 2021 Economic Census 500 m all-industry employment.
- The evidence compiler retains atomic municipalities/wards, then the national packager aggregates those controls to directed prefecture pairs. It places each cross-prefecture cohort by weighted sampling from 250 m commuter-origin sites and 500 m job sites. See [`estat_japan_prefecture.py`](../../map-creator/src/open_world_map_creator/demand/estat_japan_prefecture.py) and [`package_japan.py`](../../map-creator/src/open_world_map_creator/demand/package_japan.py).
- [`boundary_sites.py`](../../map-creator/src/open_world_map_creator/demand/boundary_sites.py) builds one owner-wide site set and assigns every source prefecture's home and job weights onto it; [`building_sites.py`](../../map-creator/src/open_world_map_creator/demand/building_sites.py) derives each canonical `demand-site-...` ID from the clustered member-building IDs. Within the cross set, [`package_japan.py`](../../map-creator/src/open_world_map_creator/demand/package_japan.py) deduplicates endpoints by that ID and accumulates cross resident/worker mass in one `cross_points` record. Non-commute cross-tile cohorts should feed this same accumulator so they enlarge or add roles to existing cross-demand dots instead of creating an overlapping non-commute point collection. Native demand may remain a separate point set and rendered layer; merging native and cross point records is outside this recommendation.
- The latest v7 generation report records 69,466,356 accepted commuter-flow mass, of which 6,038,347 is cross-prefecture, represented by all 2,082 possible non-self directed prefecture pairs and 33,553 cross-prefecture cohorts. Those counts confirm that the current published national layer is a **47×47 control**, even though its raw Census evidence was municipality-to-municipality.
- Packaged cross demand uses commute-shaped `07:30` outward and `17:30` return departures. Non-commute travel would need an explicit temporal policy rather than inheriting those times by accident.

The new source is therefore conceptually additive to the Census OD: the Census covers commuting/schooling, while the trunk survey expressly excludes it. The implementation still needs a separate ledger/category so demand can be audited and tuned independently.

## How precise are the net-flow ODs?

### Geography

The finest standard public OD is 207-zone to 207-zone. It is not municipality-to-municipality, station-to-station, or latitude/longitude. Each endpoint means “somewhere within this group of municipalities.” The zone correspondence can be joined to municipality polygons, but subdividing a released zone does not recover observed locations.

This is still materially better than prefecture controls. Nationwide there are about 4.4 zones per ordinary prefecture on average, although resolution is uneven and metropolitan aggregations can be especially coarse. Distribution across an entire prefecture would blur flows that the official source already localizes to a sub-prefectural region.

### Statistical precision

MLIT creates the net-flow estimates from mode-specific passenger surveys and operator/transport totals, expands them to the population, and reconciles transfers so one end-to-end journey is counted once. [MLIT creation summary](https://www.mlit.go.jp/statistics/details/t-ryokaku-2_tk_000006.html)

The released weekday and holiday products include OD-specific reliability tables at both 50-zone and 207-zone resolution. MLIT defines these as 95% confidence relative errors by used-mode OD: an estimate `F` with relative error `r` corresponds to an approximate interval `F ± F×r`. [MLIT data guide, pp. 41–43](https://www.mlit.go.jp/common/001005632.pdf) The rail component was designed to target an average prefecture-pair 95% relative error of 20%, but that is a design target for the rail survey, not a guarantee for every 207-zone, purpose, or mode cell. [rail survey sampling](https://www.mlit.go.jp/statistics/details/t-tetsudo-2_tk_000027.html)

Consequences:

- Treat each published count as an estimate, even when displayed as an integer.
- Small 207-zone × purpose × mode cells can have wide errors. Read and retain the matching reliability class/error rather than treating every nonzero cell equally.
- Pooling purposes or modes improves stability. A robust first release can conserve all-mode purpose totals at 207-zone OD, retain purpose, and let the game choose mode.
- A zero can mean excluded scope, especially within the three metropolitan groups or within one prefecture. It must not automatically mean observed zero travel.
- Public tables do not contain exact respondent endpoints. Even if restricted trip-level microdata exist on application, confidentiality and sampling would still prevent treating respondent points as a complete point-to-point population.

### Temporal precision

The one-day products represent particular autumn survey days (normally a Wednesday and Sunday), and MLIT warns that weather, events, operations, and survey date affect flow and purpose. Annual flows are expanded from weekday/holiday samples and transport totals and are less season-sensitive, but annual purpose tables are not released because purpose changes seasonally. [MLIT usage cautions](https://www.mlit.go.jp/statistics/details/t-ryokaku-2_tk_000008.html)

For game demand, choose explicitly between:

- an **average-day mass** (`annual flow / 365`) with purpose shares inferred from autumn weekday/holiday tables; or
- separate representative weekday/holiday demand profiles.

Do not add an outbound “trip” and invent a same-day return for all of it. The origin-to-destination tables already contain actual directional movements, including return legs. The residence-to-travel-destination tables are preferable for a home/visitor semantic because they consistently associate a resident origin with the visited region rather than reversing home and destination on the return journey. [MLIT definitions](https://www.mlit.go.jp/statistics/details/t-ryokaku-2_tk_000008.html)

## Recommended model

### Phase 1: nationwide trunk non-commute layer

Use 2015 207-zone residence-to-travel-destination tables. Keep four purposes and start with all-mode mass. For each directed zone pair:

1. Derive the daily mass from annual all-mode flow, or retain separate weekday/holiday scenarios.
2. Use autumn purpose shares only where sample reliability is adequate; otherwise shrink the cell toward its prefecture-pair or national purpose mix.
3. Generate deterministic cohorts while conserving the zone-pair total and record source vintage, source cell, purpose, and reliability metadata.
4. Allocate the origin within its 207 zone from the **origin-side distribution**. Use a total-resident 250 m mesh for resident-origin non-commute flows; the current commuter/student 250 m marginal is only a prototype fallback because it omits children, retirees, and other nonworkers.
5. Allocate the destination within its 207 zone from the **existing endpoint/worker distribution** (currently the 500 m job mesh projected onto the shared sites), then refine that distribution only with defensible purpose-specific endpoint evidence:
   - business: job density plus commercial establishments;
   - tourism: the endpoint distribution plus accommodation capacity, official attraction/visitor counts where available, and visitor-serving POIs;
   - private/homecoming: residential density where the endpoint is semantically a home, otherwise retain the endpoint distribution;
   - other/unknown: the endpoint distribution or a documented blend, never an unlabelled copy of the origin population surface.
6. Apply land/building ownership and placement constraints using the existing boundary-site pipeline, but keep the statistical claim at 207-zone resolution. Build or load the canonical owner-wide sites once, and assign commuter and non-commute origin/endpoint weights onto those same site IDs; do not independently cluster a non-commute point set.
7. Keep commuter and non-commute **cross-cohort ledgers** separate for provenance and tuning, but publish them through the existing cross-demand point catalog keyed by `Site.id`. Accumulate resident-origin and destination/worker mass by `(site ID, view role)` across both cross-demand classes before serializing `world/cross_demand.json.gz`. The cross-demand map must therefore render one feature per cross-site ID with the summed commuter plus non-commute mass. Do not create a second non-commute point collection or layer. Native demand remains independent and does not need to be coalesced with the cross set.

The project already has most of the mechanical pieces: a national directed-pair ledger, deterministic weighted endpoint pickers, fine residential and job mesh inputs, building anchoring, route enrichment, and mass-conservation audits. The new work is primarily a source adapter, 207-zone geography/crosswalk, role-correct purpose-aware weights, temporal semantics, a distinct non-commute cohort ledger, and aggregation into the existing cross-point catalog.

Add publication tests and report fields that prove the merge rather than relying on visual inspection:

- every cross-cohort endpoint resolves to exactly one canonical cross-site ID;
- a site used by both commuter and non-commute cross cohorts appears exactly once in `cross_demand.points`, with one owner and coordinate;
- for each cross site and view role, the displayed mass equals the sum of commuter and non-commute endpoint mass;
- rendered cross-point feature IDs are unique, including when one site participates in both cross-demand classes;
- total mass is conserved separately by source/purpose and jointly across all cohort classes.

### Phase 2: fill structural gaps

The first release should report the omitted mass explicitly. Use the regional Person Trip purpose × representative-mode zone OD tables for [Tokyo (2018)](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/index.php/stat-search/files?cycle=0&layout=dataset&month=0&tclass1=000001151671&toukei=00600550&tstat=000001151670&year=20181), [Chukyo (2022)](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?cycle=0&layout=datalist&stat_infid=000040268715&tclass1=000001211940&tclass2=000001227823&tclass3val=0&toukei=00600560&tstat=000001078088), and [Kinki (2021)](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?layout=dataset&stat_infid=000040170629&toukei=00600570) as the primary all-mode daily controls. Sum across representative modes, remove commute/school, reconstruct the non-commute share of return-home trips, and multiply the expanded typical-weekday estimate by 365 for the game's year. Do not rake mode shares to the Road Census, Passenger Regional Flow, or Metropolitan Transportation Census; keep those mode-specific/gross series as validation only. Extrapolate Tokyo's excluded islands and the portions of Gifu and Mie outside Chukyo PT with mode-neutral resident-generation and destination-attraction models.

### Phase 3: validate, do not stack

Use the newer tourism survey, accommodation residence matrix, common-standard visitor totals, and annual Passenger Regional Flow Survey as validation/marginal controls. They overlap the same real travel and should not be independently summed:

- Tourism survey: validate total domestic day/overnight and purpose mix.
- Accommodation survey: validate overnight destination/origin patterns after converting trips to nights consistently.
- Common-standard visitor statistics: validate destination intensity and day trips where coverage exists.
- Passenger Regional Flow Survey: compare annual transport-mode totals after translating net trips versus gross legs.

## Decision

Proceed with the user's proposal, but replace “prefecture OD then population density” with **“207-zone OD, origin-side production weights, endpoint/worker-side attraction weights, and aggregation into the existing canonical cross-point catalog.”** Prefecture OD is an acceptable minimal prototype or reliability-pooling fallback; it should not be the intended final resolution. The hard limitation is not lack of national non-commute data. It is the age and scope holes of the latest released net-flow survey, particularly its deliberate exclusion of internal three-metro flows and its lack of point endpoints.

Before implementation, download and profile the 2015 207-zone residence-to-travel-destination workbooks plus their reliability workbooks. Produce three audits before generating game cohorts: coverage by tile pair, flow mass by purpose/mode/day type, and the share of each pair in reliability bands. That will show whether the fine OD should be used directly, pooled to prefecture pairs, or partially shrunk for sparse cells.

## Amendment: mode-neutral completion of the missing prefecture pairs

The **typical weekday is suitable as the game's base day**. The regional PT tables are already expanded person-trip estimates for that day, so the game-year control is `PT weekday trips × 365`. The reasons not to add the tables naively are semantic rather than temporal:

- Their OD is an actual movement leg, whereas the 2015 preferred table is residence-to-travel-destination. A multi-stop daily tour can therefore contribute several PT legs.
- Commute and school are mixed into the published all-purpose total and must be removed to avoid duplicating the existing Census layer.
- `return home` is a separate purpose that does not say whether the outbound tour was commute, school, business, or private. Dropping it loses the return half of non-commute tours; keeping all of it reintroduces commute/school returns.
- Tokyo PT excludes Tokyo's island municipalities, while Chukyo PT covers all Aichi but only southern Gifu and northern Mie. These are coverage gaps, not time-scale problems.

The mode dimension itself is not required. Use the published all-representative-mode total, or sum mutually exclusive representative-mode rows once and then discard mode. That produces a mode-neutral person-trip control and leaves the game free to change mode share.

### Completion rule

1. Define the structural-hole set `M` as the 30 directed pairs omitted within the Tokyo, Chukyo, and Kinki groups. Preserve the raw 2015 evidence table unchanged and attach the regional PT source only to `M`.
2. From each PT OD, retain home–business, home–private, work–business, and other-private movements; remove home–work and home–school. Include unknown purpose only through an explicit proportional allocation or uncertainty band.
3. Reconstruct the non-commute share of `return home`. Prefer person/tour-level aggregation from the official custom systems or approved microdata: identify each person's home anchor and label the return leg by the tour's outbound purpose. If only aggregate OD is available, estimate the retained fraction of return flow `j→i` from the reciprocal home-based outbound mix `i→j`: `(home–business + home–private) / (home–work + home–school + home–business + home–private)`, with pooling for thin cells. Record this as an imputation rather than an observed purpose.
4. Sum across all representative modes after purpose handling. Do not preserve an observed road, rail, or bus share as a constraint. Convert the expanded daily control to the game-year scale by multiplying by 365, then apply only deterministic cohort compression—not another statistical expansion.
5. Use Tokyo PT directly for mainland Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, and Chiba; estimate the small Tokyo-island remainder from island resident population and PT purpose-specific per-resident generation rates. Use Kinki PT directly for the full Kyoto–Osaka–Hyogo–Nara group.
6. For the portions of Gifu and Mie outside Chukyo PT, extrapolate without a mode-specific survey. Fit purpose-specific daily trip-generation rates within the covered PT area by resident age/urbanity, apply them to uncovered municipal population, and distribute destinations with the same purpose-aware attraction and distance model used elsewhere. Rake the covered and extrapolated pieces only to their combined mode-neutral PT-derived total, not to Passenger Regional Flow or Road Census mode volumes.
7. The 2015 annual prefecture matrix also has 18 present-but-zero directed cells outside `M`. If the game requires positive non-commute support for every direction, fill these with a small, explicitly synthetic all-mode gravity estimate trained on positive 2015 cells, using origin population, destination attraction, distance, adjacency, and reciprocal flow. Do not use a private-car table merely to force those cells positive; retaining the official zero is also defensible if universal non-commute connectivity is not a requirement.
8.
