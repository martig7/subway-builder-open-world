# NEC non-commute passenger-flow data candidates

Date: 2026-09-24. Research scope: publicly documented evidence for directed, non-commute passenger movements that could supplement NEC's existing Census/LODES commute demand. This note does not select an implementation or change a mod.

## What the Japan and NEC builds need

Japan's existing [non-commute ledger](../../worlds/japan/data/noncommute/README.md) contains directed **one-way movements per representative weekday**. It combines a national OD survey outside three metropolitan exclusions with regional Person Trip surveys. Its sampled flows use existing canonical sites and do not add residents or workers. NEC currently has a [LODES-based demand definition](../../worlds/nec-corridor/demand.json) for home-to-work flows, with no equivalent non-commute ledger. The shared runtime already accepts `tripType: oneWay`; the missing piece is defensible evidence for **amount, direction, purpose, geography and day type**, followed by a documented conversion into those movements.

No single U.S. public source found here matches Japan's combination of purpose-specific, directed, representative-weekday totals across the whole NEC at useful local resolution. The practical choice is between a consistent but coarse national OD control and finer but patchy regional household surveys.

## National OD candidate: FHWA NextGen NHTS passenger OD

The closest publicly downloadable national analogue to Japan's one-way non-commute OD evidence is FHWA's **2022 NextGen NHTS National Passenger Origin-Destination Data**, a passive-device-derived, population-expanded **trip** table. It reports intrazonal and interzonal passenger movements for **583 FHWA zones** (state-specific metropolitan areas and non-metropolitan remainders), with origin and destination zones and annual counts. The 2021 and 2022 releases also have 12 monthly files. The latest year listed on FHWA's portal is 2022; 2020 and 2021 are useful sensitivity checks, though 2020 is pandemic-affected. [Official downloads](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/downloads), [quick-start guide](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/NextGen_NHTS_OD_Quick_Start_Guide_20231129.pdf), [release note](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/NextGen_NHTS_Passenger_OD_Technical_Release_Note.pdf).

Direct files:

- [2022 annual passenger OD ZIP, including dictionary](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/data/2022_Passenger_OD_Annual.zip)
- [2022 annual data dictionary XLSX](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/data/2022_Passenger_OD_Annual_Dictionary.xlsx)
- [2022 monthly passenger OD ZIP](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/data/2022_Passenger_OD_Monthly.zip)
- [FHWA zone shapefile ZIP](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/data/NextGen_OD_Zone_ESRI_11152022.zip)

The published attributes include total passenger trips, **work and nonwork** trips, four broad mode counts (air, rail, vehicle, active transportation/ferries), eight distance bands (0–10, 10–25, 25–50, 50–75, 75–100, 100–150, 150–300, >300 miles), plus purpose-by-distance and mode-by-distance tabulations. Rail includes Amtrak, commuter rail and subway, with access and egress linked to the true journey endpoints; vehicle also includes buses and taxis. The documentation and portal show **separate purpose and mode marginals**, but no rail-by-nonwork joint field. The downloaded dictionary should be inspected before relying on any exact column name or assuming a purpose-by-mode intersection. [FHWA documentation and FAQ](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/documentation), [portal analytics choices](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/vis/chord), [technical release note](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/NextGen_NHTS_Passenger_OD_Technical_Release_Note.pdf).

The FHWA purpose rule assigns **work** when at least one trip end is the traveler's inferred workplace; other trips are **nonwork**. Thus nonwork excludes inferred workplace travel but **includes school, shopping, social and other travel** rather than matching a strict non-commute/non-school category. The inclusion of school is an inference from the published binary definition; it is not a separately observed school label. Work and nonwork are model-imputed from phone locations, not survey answers. [FHWA purpose definition](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/documentation), [methodology](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/2020_NextGen_NHTS_Passenger_OD_Data_Methodology_v2.pdf).

**Limits for NEC:** The 583-zone system is much coarser than NEC's 59 tiles; e.g. a state-specific metropolitan zone covers many distinct neighborhoods and perhaps multiple tiles. It is suitable as a directional **metro/intercity OD control**, requiring an explicitly modeled within-zone/tile allocation. It cannot directly provide observed building, block or tile endpoints. The national files are annual/monthly, **not a weekday diary or hour-of-day series**. FHWA described weekday/weekend and hourly outputs for the separate add-on passenger OD product, not for the national release. Convert annual counts to an all-day average by dividing by 365, then use regional survey evidence to infer a typical weekday profile; dividing annual trips by only the number of weekdays would incorrectly assign weekend travel to weekdays. [Quick-start guide](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/NextGen_NHTS_OD_Quick_Start_Guide_20231129.pdf), [FHWA product-specification newsletter](https://nhts.ornl.gov/media/assets/NextGen%20NHTS_Newsletter_Issue2.pdf).

**Zeros and direction matter.** In the 2022 public files, OD pairs with 1–30 total trips have all numeric columns masked to zero and `flag=1`; a flagged zero is not a true absence of travel. The annual file is formed before monthly masking, so summing public monthly files yields less than the annual file. FHWA reports 45,048 affected annual OD pairs and 549,249 removed annual trips, and a 0.34% OD-pair directional imbalance. Do not synthesize an equal return for every observed direction or treat masked cells as confirmed zeros. The technical release note adds that an absent raw-device OD pair can mean either no real travel or sample non-observation. [2022 methodology addendum](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/2022_NextGen_NHTS_Passenger_OD_Data_Methodology_Addendum.pdf), [technical release note](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/assets/doc/NextGen_NHTS_Passenger_OD_Technical_Release_Note.pdf).

The files are publicly downloadable and FHWA publishes a preferred citation on the download page. I found no dataset-specific redistribution license stated there; verify reuse terms for a distributed mod package rather than assuming the public ZIP's availability alone settles redistribution. [Official downloads and citation](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/downloads).

| [NYC DOT 2024 Citywide Mobility Survey](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Transportation/Citywide-Mobility-Survey-Trip-2024/wgnh-qwsg) | Recent NYC-resident diary with about 116,000 public trip rows, purpose, mode, origin/destination and day-of-week fields. It is useful for purpose, time and weekday factors within NYC. | The [user guide](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/2024-cms-user-guide.pdf) says public locations are limited to CMS survey zones and trip weights represent a typical **Monday–Sunday** day. It excludes trips by non-residents, so it cannot alone estimate all inbound NEC travel. Apply appropriate weights and weekday filters. |

These surveys should **shape or validate** the FHWA control for the areas and trip classes they cover, rather than be added to it. Their sampling frames differ: for example, a household survey of NYC residents misses visitors traveling into the city. Sparse observed TAZ-pair cells should be pooled to a supportable geography before using them as absolute OD counts.

## Corridor-specific validation

The FRA's [NEC FUTURE ridership analysis](https://www.fra.dot.gov/necfuture/pdfs/tier1_deis/appendix/app_b08.pdf) estimated 2012 annual trips across its study area by auto, air, intercity rail and bus, split into business, non-business and commute purposes; its survey and model therefore offer a corridor-specific check on purpose and mode shares. The [survey report](https://www.fra.dot.gov/necfuture/pdfs/feis/volume_2/appendix/app_b08.pdf) describes 11,858 completed surveys with origin, destination, purpose and mode, but this research did not find a public machine-readable respondent OD table. The published model figures are older annual **study-area** totals, not an NEC tile ledger. Amtrak's [state station sheets](https://www.amtrak.com/state-fact-sheets) can separately check rail magnitude, but provide neither pairwise OD nor purpose.

## Evidence-to-demand trial

1. Download the FHWA 2022 annual OD ZIP, dictionary and zone geometry; inspect raw column definitions and record their hashes. Overlay the 583 zones with NEC's 59 tile footprints and report which zone pairs can be mapped, which are intrazonal, and how much volume has an endpoint outside the mod. Keep masked zeros distinct from true zeros.
2. Extract **directed nonwork** counts once per OD pair. Keep all-mode totals separate from the mode marginals. Treat an annual total divided by 365 as an all-days mean; derive a representative weekday factor and departure distribution from supported regional diary surveys. If school is excluded to match Japan's convention, document the survey-based adjustment and its uncertainty.
3. For each national OD pair, allocate to covered NEC tiles and then existing canonical sites using residential origin weights and plausible activity destination weights. Survey TAZ patterns may inform this split where available. Record every modeled split, unallocated trip, missing-geography fallback and conservation total. Do not infer exact endpoints from a metro-wide FHWA zone.
4. Publish a source ledger with source year, zone pair, original units, purpose rule, temporal factor, survey adjustments, suppression status and allocated one-way weekday movements. Compare aggregated outputs with held-out survey tabulations, FRA corridor purpose/mode totals and Amtrak boardings. Avoid counting the FHWA control and its regional refinements as separate passengers.

The first feasibility check is the **zone-to-tile overlap and volume audit**. If most target NEC travel is intrazonal within very large FHWA metro zones, the national table alone cannot determine the tile-to-tile non-commute pattern; obtaining or deriving finer regional OD controls becomes a prerequisite for a credible build.

## Preliminary recommendation

Use 2022 FHWA **nonwork** directional counts as **coarse zone-pair constraints** for a first regional/intercity scenario, preserving the source year, purpose definition, mode marginals, suppression flag and annual-trip units. Keep the Census/LODES home-to-work persons as a separate demand class: a nonwork trip is an additional movement, not necessarily an additional resident. The all-mode control can inform how much travel exists, while mode counts and Amtrak boardings provide plausibility checks; the public national FHWA fields do not directly identify *nonwork rail* trips. Allocate zone-pair counts to NEC tiles using regional person-trip surveys and local population/activity evidence, labeling the resulting tile endpoints as modeled. A typical weekday timetable and any school-trip subtraction also need regional survey evidence. Do not impose symmetric returns or force suppressed pairs positive.

The principal gap is local-resolution OD coverage for Boston, Providence and southern Connecticut, plus current travel behavior across the corridor. That gap and the FHWA zone overlap audit should determine how much of an initial release can be called survey-constrained rather than modeled.
